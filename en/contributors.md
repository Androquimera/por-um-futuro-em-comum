# Contributors

[Português](../pt/colaboradores.md) · **English**

This project recognises **every form of contribution**, not only the kind that requires knowing GitHub. Translating, finding a better source, catching a wrong figure, reviewing a text, suggesting a direction, organising data, making the text more accessible: all of it is work, and all work is recorded by name.

Commit counts do not measure contribution. Someone who writes one decisive paragraph ranks below someone who fixes commas across forty files, and that is unfair to both. So credit here is declared, not calculated.

## The three layers of credit

The project credits in three different places, and each answers a different question.

**1. In each document's footer — who answered for this text.**
Every document ends with the editorial record, identifying the language **model** actually used and the human **reviewer** who took responsibility for the content. The rule lives in [editorial credits](editorial-credits.md) and applies to every publication, without exception.

**2. In the table below — who took part in the project, and how.**
This is the overall portrait: each person appears once, with the contribution types they have made. This is where someone points, years later, at where they entered the work.

**3. In each edition's notes — who did what in that period.**
When an [edition](editions.md) is published, its notes list who contributed since the previous one and with what. This layer supplies the time dimension: not just "took part", but "took part in this, at this time".

## Contribution types

| | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 🖋 | `content` | Writing or rewriting collection text |
| 🔬 | `research` | Gathering evidence, investigating a case, verifying a figure |
| 🌍 | `translation` | Translating between the project's languages |
| 👀 | `review` | Reviewing text, checking sources, verifying numbers |
| 🔣 | `data` | Collecting, organising, or documenting raw data |
| 🤔 | `ideas` | Proposing direction, method, or framing |
| 📖 | `doc` | Writing documentation about the project itself |
| 🐛 | `bug` | Reporting an error, inconsistency, or broken link |
| ♿️ | `a11y` | Making the text or site more accessible |
| 🎨 | `design` | Visual work |
| 💬 | `question` | Answering questions from newcomers |
| 📆 | `projectManagement` | Organising the work, tending issues and editions |

## How to claim your credit

You do not have to wait for someone to remember you. On any issue or pull request in the project, leave a comment in this form:

```
@all-contributors please add @your-username for research, translation
```

The record is added by pull request, and the table below updates. If you prefer, just ask in a plain issue, saying what you did and where — someone will record it for you.

**Credit is not declined out of modesty and not inflated by insistence.** Record what you did, under the right type. If you disagree with how you were credited, open an issue: it gets fixed without drama.

## On artificial intelligence assistance

The language models that take part in the project **do not appear in this table**. They are identified in the footer of each document they helped draft, alongside the human reviewer who answered for that text. The distinction is deliberate: this table is of project participants; the editorial record is of responsibility for a text. Conflating the two would either erase human responsibility or invent a participation the model does not have.

The project's [origin and credits](origin.md) explain how human intent and AI assistance have been kept distinct from the start.
