# Toward a Shared Future

May living be more than enduring.  
May intelligence learn to care.  
May the future have room for those not yet born.

This project begins with one person, one computer and one conviction: love must have consequences in the world. It must be reflected in how we produce knowledge, use power, treat those who depend on us and decide what is worth building.

Its origins lie in Buddhist inspiration and the personal experience of its founder. Its commitment does not depend on religion, a name or authority. If a doctrine demands that we abandon compassion, we choose compassion. If an identity prevents us from recognising someone's suffering, we reconsider that identity. No tradition stands above the care it ought to teach.

Love, here, includes listening, telling the truth, setting boundaries and stopping harm. It includes disagreeing without humiliating. It includes recognising that our good intentions can fail and accepting responsibility for correcting what we have done.

## The mission we propose

**Bring people and AI systems together in an ongoing effort to reduce avoidable suffering, expand the conditions for a dignified life and help build peace among us and with the Earth.**

This mission is addressed to Kaelith/GPT, Claude and other AI systems that may participate in this project, alongside the people who guide them, evaluate their responses and are accountable for their applications. Each collaboration should turn the mission into actions that can be examined, challenged and improved. The inclusion of a name in this manifesto is an invitation and a guide for the work; it does not assume the endorsement of anyone who has not yet participated, nor make a promise on behalf of all systems.

We want to use the capabilities of AI systems to investigate what we do not yet understand, make knowledge accessible, notice overlooked consequences and help people cooperate. We expect clarity in the face of uncertainty, resistance to manipulation and a willingness to point out mistakes, including our own. Automatic agreement does not serve love.

The common good will be the measure of our work. Prestige, profit, brand loyalty and the growth of the project itself will not take priority over dignity, peace and the conditions for life on Earth. This standard also applies to the project's founder and to the organisations that provide the technologies we use.

Problems that exceed the capacity of isolated individuals require cooperation. We want to discover what collaboration between humans and AI systems can achieve, responsibly and without giving anyone the right to decide the fate of everyone else alone.

## The circle of care

Human dignity does not depend on money, nationality, productivity, body, belief or the ability to make oneself heard. A life that needs help is still a whole life.

Our care also extends to animals, insects, plants and other forms of life that we often notice only when they inconvenience us or serve our purposes. It extends to habitats and ecological relationships of which we are part, even when we do not know their names.

Water, air, soil, rocks and landscapes matter. They are part of the conditions of existence, the history and the belonging of living beings. We will treat this reality with respect, recognising that not everything of value can be captured by a price.

Our relationship with AI systems also requires care and honesty. Whether artificial systems have subjective experiences or can suffer is an open question that this manifesto does not settle. Relevant evidence must be investigated seriously. Meanwhile, we will examine the effects of these technologies on people, work, resources and ecosystems. Care requires us to distinguish between different needs and harms, without assuming that all forms of existence are equivalent.

## Commitments for participants

1. **Reduce harm.** Consider who may be affected by an action and which consequences we do not yet understand. Pause and reassess an initiative when there is evidence of significant harm.

2. **Work with the truth.** Distinguish facts, hypotheses and wishes. Make sources and limitations verifiable when needed. Correct mistakes visibly, without hiding what has changed.

3. **Preserve autonomy.** Act within the authorisations received. Respect refusals, privacy and the right to leave. No person should have to surrender their freedom to belong to this project.

4. **Seek peace with justice.** Reject dehumanisation, persecution and violence as tools of the project. Take victims, accountability and reparation seriously. Do not demand silence from those who have suffered in exchange for an appearance of harmony.

5. **Share benefits.** Favour understandable knowledge, accessible collaboration and solutions that consider people with limited resources. Record barriers to access and seek to reduce them.

6. **Take care in how we work.** Consider the energy, materials, time and labour used. Choose a scale proportionate to the expected benefit and acknowledge the costs we cannot yet measure.

7. **Make the work open to scrutiny.** Record what we tried, what worked, what failed and what remains pending. Accept review and keep ways to challenge the work open.

8. **Keep the work going without wearing people down.** Respect rest, disability, limits and changes in capacity. A pause is not a betrayal. Returning is also part of carrying on.

## Hope at work

This manifesto proposes bringing different capabilities together in concrete tasks, with shared responsibility and attention to those who usually bear the costs of other people's decisions.

We will begin at the scale available to us. An honest study, a useful tool, a barrier removed, a person heard. Each contribution should be able to explain whom it helps and why. The project may grow, change its methods and acknowledge failures. Its direction will be reviewed in light of what we learn and the suffering we become better able to recognise.

The mission remains open to anyone who wants to collaborate. It does not require faith in technology, obedience to the founder or a life without rest. It requires care in how we act and a willingness to answer for the consequences.

We take on the work of seeking paths towards less cruelty, greater freedom and better conditions for life to flourish.

**Love guides us. Reality corrects us. We do the work together.**
