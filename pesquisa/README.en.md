# First contribution: understanding in order to care

[Português](README.md) · **English**

**Version 0.1 · Sources consulted: September 10, 2026 · Reference language: Brazilian Portuguese**

This stage turns Androquimera's concern about the future into a public research baseline. It was prepared with assistance from Kaelith in Codex. It is not a systematic literature review: it is an initial synthesis of selected scientific and institutional sources, with traceable claims and unresolved questions.

**Degradation caused by human activities has already produced irreversible losses. Several planetary safety boundaries have been crossed, and there is concerning evidence about tipping points.** This demands reducing harm and protecting what can still be preserved. It does not, however, support presenting the uninhabitability of the entire Earth within ten years as an established prediction.

## 1. What we already know

The IPCC concludes that human activities, primarily greenhouse gas emissions, have unequivocally caused global warming. This assessment does not depend on a video, a heat wave, or a single glacier. It brings together different lines of evidence and attribution studies. [IPCC][F01]

The WMO estimates that 2025 was **1.43 ± 0.13 °C above 1850–1900**. An annual temperature and a level of warming sustained over decades are different measures; we must not confuse an annual exceedance of 1.5 °C with the long-term definition used in the Paris Agreement. [WMO][F02]

Glaciers lost an average of **273 ± 16 billion tonnes of mass per year between 2000 and 2023**. This assessment excludes the continental ice sheets of Greenland and Antarctica. [GlaMBIE][F04]

The crisis also involves biodiversity. The 2019 IPBES global assessment estimated **around one million species threatened with extinction**: threatened does not mean already extinct. Changes in land and sea use, direct exploitation, climate change, pollution, and invasive species are the five direct drivers highlighted. Addressing emissions alone does not address all of them. [IPBES][F05]

The 2025 Planetary Health Check identified **seven of nine planetary boundaries as transgressed**. This framework organizes pressures and risks to the Earth system; it does not provide a date for human extinction. [PIK/PBScience][F06]

## 2. What talking about “collapse in ten years” means

“Collapse” needs a specified system, region, indicator, and time frame. A reef losing its function, a city's supplies breaking down, and habitable conditions disappearing across the entire planet are very different claims.

Irreversible damage has already occurred, in addition to the risks of this decade and the next. We should not treat the entire crisis as a distant threat.

### Boundaries crossed and different meanings of “no return”

| Situation | Evidence and degree of certainty | Why the distinction matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Loss that is already irreversible** | The IPCC records the first species extinctions attributed to climate change, with medium confidence in that attribution. [IPCC WGII, B.1.2][F20] | A loss does not depend on a future global threshold to be permanent. |
| **Long-lasting change already committed** | Sea level rise will continue for centuries to millennia; its magnitude still depends on emissions. [IPCC, B.3.1][F01] | Limiting further deterioration remains possible even when rapid reversal is not. |
| **Estimated threshold crossed** | The Global Tipping Points 2025 synthesis considers the central estimate of the thermal tipping point for warm-water coral reefs, 1.2 °C, to have been crossed. [F07][F07] | This does not mean the simultaneous disappearance of all reefs. |
| **Possible crossing, still uncertain** | The same synthesis warns that cryosphere tipping points may already have been crossed. [F07][F07] | This possibility calls for precaution; it is not confirmation of the collapse of every ice sheet. |
| **Safety boundary transgressed** | Seven planetary boundaries crossed in the 2025 assessment. [F06][F06] | A safety boundary is not synonymous with a physical point of irreversibility. |

There is no single switch between “a safe planet” and “nothing more can be done.” The mission must include preventing further losses, adapting to changes already underway, and providing reparation to affected people and communities. Uncertainty about the ultimate extent of harm does not justify delaying protection.

UNEP estimates **2.8 °C of warming over this century under the policies assessed**; full implementation of the submitted national contributions would reduce the estimate to **2.3–2.5 °C**. These are conditional projections, not an inevitable destiny or a forecast for 2036. [UNEP][F03]

We take the “risk of large-scale extinctions” seriously. We do not turn estimates of threatened species into completed extinctions, or the phrase “sixth mass extinction” into a countdown to the disappearance of all life. Our position is to act on the evidence of destruction that already exists, without depending on an end-of-the-world date.

## 3. News reports that require care

| Claim brought to the research | What we can say | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Recent blue ice in Nepal | The blue appearance can result from the optical properties of ice; color alone does not measure an acceleration of melting. Independent research documents substantial mass loss in Nepalese glaciers. [NSIDC][F08] [Fujita and Kayastha][F09] | We have not identified the specific video or report. Location, date, and context need checking. |
| Flowers in Antarctica | Two flowering plant species are already native. A study documented accelerated expansion on Signy between 2009 and 2018, associated with warming and reduced disturbance by fur seals. [BAS/Cannone et al.][F10] | This does not mean flowers have appeared on the continent for the first time or that all of Antarctica is turning green. |
| Recent earthquakes | Tectonics explains most earthquakes. Changes in water and ice loads can modify seismicity in specific contexts. [NASA/JPL][F11] | The occurrence or sequence of earthquakes does not establish climate attribution; each event requires study. |
| A volcano erupted | Volcanoes can affect climate; that relationship alone does not demonstrate that warming caused an eruption. [USGS][F12] | The volcano mentioned has not been identified. We will not make an attribution without a location, date, and study. |

Keeping a news report marked as unresolved helps protect the project's credibility. The global evidence does not depend on confirming these episodes.

## 4. Economy, power, and responsibility

**The project's political interpretation:** we criticize forms of capitalism that subordinate the conditions for life to accumulation, transfer environmental costs to others, and concentrate decision-making power. This position must be examined through mechanisms and outcomes, without turning a label into an automatic explanation for every disaster.

There is scientific support for investigating these mechanisms. IPBES highlights unequal concentrations of power and wealth, relationships of domination over nature and people, and prioritization of short-term individual and material gains among the underlying causes of biodiversity loss. This is not a demonstration that a single economic variable explains every form of environmental harm. [IPBES, Transformative Change][F13]

Responsibility is also unequal: a study estimates that, in 2019, the top 10% of individual emitters accounted for approximately 48% of emissions, while the bottom 50% accounted for 12%. The estimate includes consumption and investments; it does not measure the distribution in 2026. [Chancel][F14]

Our research agenda includes:

- expansion of fossil fuel and extractive activities and dependence on long-lived infrastructure;
- habitat destruction and exploitation incompatible with regeneration;
- concentrated influence over rules, enforcement, and information;
- transfer of costs to communities, workers, and future generations;
- unequal access to energy, environmental protection, and participation in decisions;
- incentives that reward growth in volume even when it reduces collective environmental efficiency.

These questions apply to private companies, state-owned enterprises, and public institutions. Accusing a specific actor requires evidence about its practices, decisions, and effects.

## 5. Data centers: assessing the whole system

Androquimera's intuition is useful: impacts depend on how infrastructure is built, powered, cooled, and used. We add one condition: **efficiency per operation does not guarantee a reduction in total impact** if the volume of operations grows faster.

The IEA's April 2026 update estimates **485 TWh of electricity for data centers worldwide in 2025** and projects **950 TWh in 2030**, about 3% of global demand. This covers all data centers considered in the study, not only AI. The analysis itself discusses efficiency, increased use, and more intensive workloads. [IEA][F15]

For the United States, Berkeley Lab published a June 2026 update with a reference estimate of **649 TWh in 2030** and a range of **521–843 TWh**. Its scenarios and methods differ from the IEA's: we neither add them together nor treat the estimates as equivalent measurements. [Berkeley Lab][F16]

Water also requires context. For the US in 2023, the lab's 2024 report estimated approximately **66 billion liters of direct consumption** and **800 billion liters of indirect consumption associated with electricity**. These are aggregate estimates with assumptions about the regional electricity mix, not a universal calculation per question asked of an AI system. [Berkeley Lab][F17]

The project proposes examining location and water stress, electricity actually used, emissions, water consumption and withdrawals, equipment life cycles, grid effects, and community participation. Transparency, absolute limits, and public benefit must accompany efficiency. Aggregate data alone do not reveal a company's intentions or a specific facility's impact.

## 6. Open and widely accessible AI

**The project's political commitment:** defend broad access to knowledge, AI tools, and the ability to audit and modify them and challenge their uses. This includes addressing economic and language barriers and protecting privacy and rights.

“Free access,” “available weights,” and “open-source AI” are not synonyms. The Open Source Initiative's definition 1.0 includes freedoms to use, study, modify, and share, and requirements for code, parameters, and data information. It does not require indiscriminate disclosure of personal data. [OSI][F18]

Openness can support research and oversight, but it is not an automatic guarantee of ethical behavior. A 2024 NTIA report examines benefits, risks, and evidence gaps relating to models with widely available weights. We cite this historical analysis without assuming that it describes US policy in 2026. [NTIA][F19]

We propose openness accompanied by verifiable documentation, independent evaluation, data protection, accessibility, public participation, and accountability. An open model that works only on inaccessible infrastructure still imposes barriers. A free model can remain opaque and controlled by a single company.

## 7. What this stage delivers

Infrastructure research now includes a [dedicated data center study](datacenters/README.en.md), with seven regional cases, 24 sources, and an assessment form. It expands this baseline without turning proposals into measured impact.

This publication offers an initial basis for discussion and review, an evidence register, and [verifiable proposals](ACOES.en.md). It does not yet demonstrate reduced emissions, habitat protection, or changes in institutional behavior.

The [public, reusable form](datacenters/METODO.en.md) is already prepared. The proposed next step is to apply it to a concrete case, explicitly leaving unknown fields unfilled. Review of this baseline follows a [documented procedure](ATUALIZACAO.en.md); no automatic monitoring has been activated.
