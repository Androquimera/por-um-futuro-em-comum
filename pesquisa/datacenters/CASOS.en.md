# Ten regional cases: evidence, conflict, and response

**2026-09-10 · Version 0.2 · Selected cases, not a representative sample**

Impartiality means applying the same standard of proof to companies, governments, and criticism. It does not require giving equal weight to an account, measured data, and a judgment. The dates below delimit what was actually documented; they do not certify each development's status today.

## C01 — Virginia, United States: demand, tariffs, and neighbors

**Verifiable basis.** In May 2026, the EIA reported growth of almost 30 million MWh in commercial electricity sales between 2019 and 2025, driven primarily by data centers but also by other loads. The 2025 data were preliminary. We do not treat the entire difference as consumption exclusive to the sector. [D09][D09]

**Cost and limitation.** In 2024, JLARC considered existing cost allocation appropriate, but projected an additional US$14–37/month for a typical Dominion residential customer by 2040, in real terms, for generation and transmission. This is not an already observed increase. [D08][D08]

**Uncompromising question:** who pays for idle infrastructure if promised demand does not materialize? Requiring contracts, guarantees, and tariff assessment is more informative than assuming any price increase was caused by a single facility.

## C02 — Ireland: national concentration and local experience

**Verifiable basis.** In July 2026, the CSO reported that data centers accounted for **23% of metered electricity in 2025**, compared with 22% in 2024. The boundary is metered electricity, not all national energy. [D10][D10]

**Reporting and response.** In June 2026, ABC reported residents' concerns about resources and costs, environmental organizations' criticism, and the government's position that growth was being managed. The article used 21% and cited civil-society organizations' estimate about tariffs. We use the subsequent official statistics for the current share and do not treat the tariff estimate as a causal measurement performed by this project. [D11][D11]

**Uncompromising question:** what additional effect will the next development have on generation, transmission, emissions, and prices, considering facilities already authorized?

## C03 — Cerrillos, Chile: environmental assessment may require redesign

**Documented decision.** In February 2024, the Second Environmental Court partially annulled approval and required reassessment of water impacts considering climate change. The court also recorded a proposal to replace water-based towers with air cooling. The judgment does not establish that an operating facility caused Chile's drought. [D12][D12]

**Subsequent development.** In September 2024, T13/Reuters reported that Google would not proceed with the project as originally submitted and would redesign it. We do not confuse this development with another facility in Quilicura. We have not confirmed a subsequent new permit or operation here. [D13][D13]

**Uncompromising question:** does the hydrogeological model use the worst plausible period of availability, or an average that hides drought?

## C04 — Pecém, Ceará, Brazil: energy benefits do not replace participation

**Community evidence.** The Anacé protocol and residents' mobilization are now included in [Communities](COMUNIDADES.en.md), before assessing institutional responses.

**Documented act.** MPF/DPU issued a recommendation in May 2026 identifying weaknesses in licensing, calling for consultation with the Anacé people and other traditional communities and assessment of cumulative impacts. This is an extrajudicial act, not a judgment of liability or demonstration of operational harm already inflicted. [D14][D14]

**Operator's response.** In a statement published by O POVO, Omnia asserted licensing compliance, closed-circuit cooling, renewable energy, and projected consumption below **30 m³/day**. We record this as a company statement and forecast, not audited consumption or proof of no aquifer impact. [D16][D16]

**Economic context.** Reuters reported a **US$2 billion, 20-year** renewable supply agreement with Casa dos Ventos, announced by the companies. The contract alone does not resolve environmental and participation questions. [D15][D15]

**Uncompromising question:** where are the studies, baseline data, response to the recommendations, and consultation record? The documentation gathered does not yet allow us to definitively declare these requirements fulfilled or breached.

## C05 — Johor, Malaysia: construction, water, and disputed causation

**Documented reporting.** In July 2026, Reuters reported protests and complaints about construction, dust, and water pressure. Residents reported problems before the facility began operating. ZDATA denied causing the water problems and stated that it exclusively used treated wastewater. The article documents the conflict; it does not resolve hydraulic causation. [D17][D17]

**Available rule.** MIDA's guideline includes energy, carbon, and water indicators and conditions related to the DESAC incentive. An incentive document does not prove that every facility meets identical standards or replace local enforcement. [D18][D18]

**Uncompromising question:** are there measurements of pressure, withdrawals, and discharge before and after construction, at comparable points? Without them, neither the allegation nor the denial closes the investigation.

## C06 — Singapore: conditioned expansion, without assuming zero impact

**Documented policy.** IMDA's 2024 roadmap proposed at least **300 MW** of additional capacity, with efficiency, energy, and water as key areas. Planned capacity is not energy consumed or an environmental benefit already delivered. [D19][D19]

**Recent update.** On September 9, 2026, MDDI reaffirmed room for expansion and upgrades to existing facilities and cited a newly introduced bill to raise sustainability standards. We do not present the legislative proposal as a law already in force. [D20][D20]

**Uncompromising question:** do the targets reduce absolute pressure on resources, or merely make expansion more efficient while its total effect continues increasing?

## C07 — Odense, Denmark: useful heat requires infrastructure and users

**Benefit reported by the operator.** In January 2025, Fjernvarme Fyn stated that recovered heat from Meta's data center supplied approximately **11 thousand homes**. The source operates the service and has an interest in the topic; this information is not an independent audit by our project. [D21][D21]

**Interpretation.** This illustrates possible integration between digital infrastructure and heating. To compare alternatives, we need heat actually delivered, pump energy, losses, seasonality, and the fuel displaced. The number of homes does not demonstrate climate neutrality of the entire facility.

**Uncompromising question:** does the system reduce emissions and costs relative to the alternative that would actually exist there, and who receives the savings?

## C08 — Memphis / Southaven, United States: noise and cumulative exposure

Residents' accounts, community monitoring, and the company's response are examined in [Communities](COMUNIDADES.en.md). The [data register](DADOS.en.md) distinguishes published monitoring aggregates from source attribution. On-site generation means the investigation must follow electricity beyond the data center building.

## C09 — The Dalles, United States: water and access to information

The [community investigation](COMUNIDADES.en.md) follows residents' questions and local reporting to council records. The [public water series](DADOS.en.md) preserves the numeric source and calculations, including a discrepancy between reporting and data. Resource pressure and transparency are separate questions that both require answers.

## C10 — Querétaro, Mexico: expansion amid water insecurity

The [community investigation](COMUNIDADES.en.md) includes residents interviewed by Pública/CLIP, their water-access difficulties, and expansion decisions. Pre-existing scarcity requires scrutiny of new demand; it does not establish each facility's share in shortages.

## What these cases support

- Effects are concentrated geographically; global averages do not describe every community's experience.
- Companies, residents, and authorities must be tested against evidence appropriate to each claim.
- Design changes, reduced consumption, and useful recovery can matter, but require verifiable outcomes and boundaries.
- Benefits and harms need not affect the same people or occur in the same period.
- The selection covers the Americas, Europe, and Asia. It does not sufficiently cover Africa, Oceania, or every facility type.

The questions above are the project's research proposals, not answers already demonstrated by the cited documents.
