## Question and scope

How do data centers currently use resources and affect people, and which differences in design, location, and governance change those effects?

The study brings together 24 sources and seven regional cases to explain mechanisms and contrast evidence. It is a question-led narrative review, not a systematic review or an estimate of the global prevalence of harm. The scope includes operation, construction, electricity, water, equipment, neighbors, and institutions.

## How sources were selected

Searches in Portuguese and English combined data centers with energy, water, cooling, noise, communities, and regional names. Discovery used Exa/Agent Reach and web search; reading prioritized documents from the responsible institutions and original publications. Reporting was used to locate conflicts, statements, and subsequent developments.

We prioritized statistics with defined boundaries; studies with methods; audit documents and official decisions; identified reporting and responses from the parties. We included favorable and unfavorable findings. A Reuters republication is not a second independent investigation of the same story.

We excluded unsupported causal inferences, universal per-prompt comparisons, forecasts treated as observations, drafts presented as final standards, and blocked sources whose relevant contents could not be checked. The catalog records when reading was limited to an abstract, section, or institutional communication.

## Classifying each claim

| Type | What it supports | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Observation / statistics | An indicator was recorded using a particular method and period. | That the cause was isolated from every other factor. |
| Estimate / modeling | A value results from declared data and assumptions. | That it was directly measured at every facility. |
| Projection | An outcome may occur under a scenario. | That the outcome has already occurred or is inevitable. |
| Official act | An authority decided, recommended, or proposed something. | That a recommendation is a judgment of liability or a bill is enacted law. |
| Account / statement | A person or organization presented that experience or position. | That the contents were fully confirmed by an independent source. |
| Interpretation / proposal | The project advocates a line of reasoning or action. | That the action was implemented or its benefit measured. |

## Reusable form: one facility or service

Fill in only available information. Use **not available** for gaps, without substituting zero.

| Field | Required record |
|---|---|
| Identification | Facility, operator, client when known, municipality, country, and stage: announced, permitted, construction, or operation. |
| Period and boundary | Dates, buildings included, installed capacity, average load, and sources of values. |
| Electricity | Total MWh, hourly profile, MW peaks, occupancy, measurement or projection, and PUE with denominator. |
| Supply | Grid, on-site generation, contracts, hourly matching, guarantees, and connection/reinforcement costs. |
| Emissions | Operational, location-based and market-based electricity where applicable, supply chain, and possible overlaps. |
| Water | Withdrawals and consumption, source, basin, dry season, effluent, reuse, WUE, cooling method, and scarcity scenario. |
| Heat and noise | Heat destination, actual recovery, temperatures, acoustic measurements with time, distance, and tonal characteristics. |
| Construction and equipment | Previous land use, dust, traffic, drainage, equipment lifetime, repair, reuse, and waste destination. |
| People | Affected communities, consultation, complaints, response measures, and information access. |
| Economy | Temporary/permanent jobs, revenue, incentives, public costs, and who receives benefits. |
| Evidence and response | Link and date for each claim, method, parties' responses, conflicts of interest, and gaps. |
| Conclusion | What was demonstrated, what is plausible, and what remains unknown. |

To claim local causation, seek temporal comparison, a reference area, concurrent changes, and a plausible physical mechanism. For tariffs, compare scenarios and allocation rules. For water and noise, obtain appropriate before-and-after measurements. This study did not perform those investigations.

## How to check an environmental claim

1. Define the promise: which resource, facility, period, and population?
2. Locate the original source and check whether the value is measured, estimated, or projected.
3. Check units and denominators; separate consumption from withdrawals and power from energy.
4. Look for data that could contradict the claim and a response from the party concerned.
5. Assess effects beyond the building: electricity grid, basin, supply chain, and communities.
6. Explain limitations without filling gaps with certainty; publish the conclusion and what is missing.

We award no “green seal” or single score. A facility may improve water use while worsening electricity impacts, or generate municipal revenue with costs in another region. These outcomes must remain visible.

## Updates and responsibility

Review before using a case for new action and when new statistics, official responses, or changes to the development emerge. Updates are manual; no automatic monitoring has been activated.

Every change must reach Portuguese and English in the same delivery, preserving IDs, sources, numbers, and caveats. Identify the model actually used, the human reviewer/editor, and the actual review status. Record changes in CONTINUIDADE and verify the remote result when publishing.
