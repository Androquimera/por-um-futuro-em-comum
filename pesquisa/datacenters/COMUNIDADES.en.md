## The necessary correction

The initial version included criticism and reporting but favored technical and government institutions. It lacked documents produced by communities and data obtained in response to their questions. At Androquimera's request, we have corrected that choice: investigating consequences for residents now guides collection instead of merely illustrating an institutional synthesis.

Public agencies may regulate while also promoting investment. Companies hold essential information and have an economic stake in the outcome. NGOs have missions and positions; these should be understood, not used to dismiss their work. Affected people are primary sources on their experiences and may produce measurements, documents, and territorial knowledge. We assess each claim against its evidence without granting automatic credibility to a category.

## Pecém: the community defines how it wants to be consulted

The Anacé people's protocol distinguishes information meetings from collective decisions. Pages 16 and 18–19 reserve decisions for the General Assembly and provide for meeting records, complete information, and options to accept, amend, or reject. This community-authored document supplies concrete criteria for examining whether a “listening” process respected the process chosen by the people. [D25][D25]

Intercept Brasil covered the August 2025 protest, reproduced JAPIMAN's complaint about lack of consultation, and interviewed Chief Roberto Anacé. Semace responded by announcing hearings as an installation condition. Holding a meeting does not, by itself, demonstrate compliance with the protocol. [D26][D26]

Idec places the case within disputes over territory, transparency, and energy infrastructure. Its report declares support for Anacé mobilization and identifies Luminate support. This is a contribution from an NGO with an explicit position; its references must be traced, and reporting also cited here does not become independent evidence when repeated in the report. [D27][D27]

**Conclusion:** the documented problem includes participation and information access before operation. Water risks must be investigated without inventing measurements of operational harm. The company's response and MPF/DPU recommendation remain in [case C04](CASOS.en.md).

## Memphis and Southaven: residents also produce evidence

The Mississippi Free Press recorded accounts of persistent noise inside homes and difficulty sleeping, alongside opposition at a hearing. These are identified residents' experiences, not merely speculation about future projects. The report records the regulator's position and says the company had not responded to requests for comment by publication. [D29][D29]

Memphis Community Against Pollution and CEEJH INC organized community particulate monitoring. Available aggregates and their limits appear in [DADOS](DADOS.en.md). This expands investigation beyond what operators and regulators chose to measure. [D30][D30]

In July 2026, the company itself stated that there were 69 mobile turbines and announced removal by July 2027, with permanent generation replacing them. It also reported investment in controls and sound barriers. These are commitments and statements, not evidence that noise has stopped or removal is complete. [D32][D32]

**Conclusion:** this case requires studying the generation supplying the data center as well as the building. Accounts of suffering do not require a specific disease diagnosis to warrant a response; clinical attribution and pollution shares require their own investigation. Other industrial sources in the region belong in cumulative assessment, not as an excuse to ignore a new source.

## The Dalles: secrecy is part of the problem

A resident's submission attached to the November 2021 council minutes challenges the withholding of water figures and demands disclosure before the decision. Those minutes also record that Google paid for studies and would cover confidentiality litigation costs. This document allows scrutiny of interests and decisions, beyond sustainability statements. [D36][D36]

OPB's regional investigation connects water demand, planned reservoir expansion, and environmental concerns. The city and Google denied that expansion was being undertaken to serve the company. Follow-up reporting publishes supporting evidence and corrections; both stories belong to the same investigation. [D33][D33] [D34][D34]

We obtained the investigation's public CSV. The series, units, calculations, and a discrepancy found in the reporting appear in [DADOS](DADOS.en.md). The recorded increase in Google's municipal water use is verifiable; it does not establish that the company caused every supply problem or infrastructure project. [D35][D35]

**Conclusion:** delayed transparency prevents informed participation. Requiring documents before approving new water-supply commitments is the project's proposal, grounded in the documented conflict.

## Querétaro: start with residents' taps

The Pública/CLIP investigation describes Sandra García's routine to obtain water in Viborillas and data center expansion in an already stressed region. It documents information requests and company responses. This puts water access and resource distribution at the center; it does not isolate how much of each shortage was caused by each facility. [D28][D28]

**Conclusion:** a pre-existing problem does not make new demand irrelevant. The question is whether allocation and expansion decisions worsen vulnerability, with what guarantees and priorities for those already facing restrictions.

## What changes in the investigation

1. Seek communities, local organizations, and reporting before drafting a case's conclusion.
2. Treat community documents and participatory measurements as evidence according to their content, without requiring government endorsement for inclusion.
3. Identify who funded studies, who holds the data, and who was excluded from decisions.
4. Publish accessible numbers and information gaps. Missing measurements do not mean zero impact.
5. Require proportionate evidence for company promises and official assurances too. An institutional response does not close a complaint.
6. Distinguish preventive protection, documented harm, and causal attribution without postponing all care until impossible certainty.

This expansion adds 12 sources to the initial 24 and three cases to the previous seven. It does not represent every affected community. We did not interview people at this stage; we used their public documents and previously published interviews, with attribution.
