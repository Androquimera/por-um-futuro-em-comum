# How to keep the research up to date

[Português](ATUALIZACAO.md) · **English**

**Initial procedure: 2026-09-10 · Proposed next editorial review: 2026-10-10**

The baseline is revisable, and at this stage updates are manual. **No monitoring, scheduling, or automatic collection has been activated.** The proposed date organizes a future resumption; it is not a promise of background execution.

## When to review

- At a monthly review, if participants are available.
- When new editions of the cited assessments, corrections, retractions, or relevant data appear.
- When a source arrives for episodes that remain unidentified.
- Before using a claim as the basis for new public action.

Annual indicators do not need rewriting with every daily news report. A regional update does not automatically replace a global series.

## Procedure for humans, Claude, and other assistants

1. Read the project's guidance, this baseline, and the current request. Check Git and other people's changes.
2. Open the original source and record **publication date, data period, region, unit, scenario, and consultation date**. Distinguish an article's publication from its receipt.
3. Look for a newer edition, errata, and methodological limitations. Prioritize papers, data, and institutions responsible for the assessment. News reports help locate the origin; they do not replace its content.
4. Update or add the relevant row in both versions of EVIDENCIAS. Preserve the ID, record what changed, and keep different periods distinguishable.
5. Review affected conclusions in both versions of README and ACOES. A change that weakens our argument must also be incorporated.
6. Add or correct the source entry in both versions of FONTES. State whether an abstract, section, table, or full text was read; do not invent a complete review.
7. Update Portuguese and English in full in the same delivery. Check parity of content, IDs, sources, numbers, dates, units, and caveats, as well as internal links and the diff. Avoid private data and text copied from sources. Do not publish with a missing or outdated version.
8. Update both versions of CONTINUIDADE with the delivery, verification, limitations, and next step. Publish when authorized by the current task; verify the remote commit after pushing.
9. Identify the model used and the reviewer in each publication, following [RESPONSABILIDADE.en.md](../RESPONSABILIDADE.en.md), always in both languages.

## Rules that prevent false conclusions

- The report's year is not necessarily the year of the data.
- An isolated event does not establish a trend; a trend does not automatically attribute an event.
- Estimates, measurements, and projections play different roles.
- Annual warming and sustained long-term warming are not interchangeable.
- Glaciers and ice sheets, threats and extinctions, water consumption and withdrawal are not synonyms.
- Global, national, sectoral, and individual facility totals must not be mixed.
- Demand from all data centers is not equivalent to AI demand.
- Do not produce “liters per prompt” from aggregate totals without valid boundaries and methods.
- Missing information must appear as **not available**, never as zero.
- Do not add scenarios from different institutions or present them as a numerical consensus.
- Do not interpret relative efficiency as a reduction in absolute impact.
- Do not confuse an author's statement, the project's interpretation, and a policy proposal.
